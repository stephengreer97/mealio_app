The recipe's amount and the shopper's count are different numbers

qty/measure is what the recipe asks for. productQty is how many packages to buy. They are only ever equal on a countable line. This is not a new heuristic — it is the rule IngredientEditor.fromFormIng already writes with (countable ⇒ qty: N, productQty: N; measured ⇒ qty: 1, productQty: 1) and the rule canonicalizeIngredient writes with on the server, read back out. A row that lost its productQty derives the number the editor would have stored.

Why the countable list is an allow-list, and a short one

A deny-list can never be finished, and every noun missing from it would be an over-buy. A name missing from the allow-list is merely a 1 — the failure the shopper can see and fix, on a screen built to ask.

The bar for entry is that buying N of it cannot over-charge: three lemons is three lemons at every store we support. Items sold loose or bagged are left out on purpose — carrots and potatoes are the near misses, and "3 carrots" fetching three 1 lb bags is exactly the outcome the list exists to avoid. pepper sits in the disqualifiers rather than the countables for the same reason: bell peppers are sold singly, but the word attaches to too many things that are not, and losing the bell peppers to a 1 is the cheap mistake.

Package-ish words with no vocabulary behind them — "bottle", "jar", "pkg", "head", "rack" — also resolve to 1. Buying N of those is often right, but "often" is not good enough for a number that spends money: a can sold as a 4-pack turns 2 into 8. One is the reading that can only ever be too small, and the stepper is right there to raise it.

Recovering the rest of these counts is catalogue work, not a parse: whether a given product is sold loose or in a bag is store data the app does not have.

Three key names for one field

DB rows carry ingredientName, productName, product_name or name depending on which writer last touched them — web, mobile, the creator portal, or a preset import. Everything reads through normIngName / normalizeIngredients, never the raw key.

prep is read off the row with a spreadable (prepOf) so that "no preparation" stays absent rather than becoming null — a row with none has to serialise exactly like one written before the field existed. It is also kept structurally away from ingredientName and searchTerm, which are what the cart searches for: nothing that builds a store query reads it.

normProductQty clamps non-positive or invalid values to 1, because saved meal data can leak qty: 0 from the chooser flow — which would otherwise disable the cart button entirely.

Trending is cached; the other two sorts are not

Trending is computed by the get_preset_meals_with_trending RPC and cached server-side for 10 minutes under the tag trending-meals, invalidated whenever a creator creates, updates or deletes a meal. "New" and "Following" query the database directly. The cache is server-side only — nothing in the app holds it.
